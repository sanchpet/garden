In its current form the site is meant as an engineer’s workbook with printed text (a compromise for readability) and handwritten notes in the margins. On the whole I try to lay everything out here like in a big garage, giving each section some physicality: a bookcase with books, a tech radar, a race calendar.

Technical details

The site is built on a fork of Quartz v5 with a pile of plugins. Yes, I write the code with AI, since web programming and frontend have never been my strong side. Formulas: KaTeX, diagrams: Mermaid, other drawings I make in Excalidraw and embed as SVG, or draw with a stylus on an iPad in Goodnotes. For a typesetting demo, go here (in Russian).

The site has a feed (RSS) in Atom. I run analytics on a self-hosted GoatCounter without cookies.

The site is bilingual, so almost every page has a twin (you can see it in the URL: /ru/ and /en/). That’s costly to maintain (though easier with LLMs), so there may be gaps in this scheme. I haven’t yet weaned myself off writing in Russian (luckily for my friends and family), but eventually the site will most likely come to English.

I write texts in Obsidian and generally try to work only through it, loading it with tons of plugins. A local plugin was put together for publishing this site too.

Weight

Heavy graphics like the graph load on demand, and the fonts are trimmed. Files are compressed ahead of time with brotli (it packs text tighter than gzip), and Caddy serves them, since it handles precompressed files out of the box, with no custom server build.

Licence

The texts are licensed under CC BY 4.01 (except other people’s pictures and quotations; code samples are 0BSD). The engine’s code is under MIT (since that’s Quartz’s original licence).

Typography

  • Text and headings: Literata
  • Block labels and lettering on drawings: Caveat
  • Quotations and margin notes: Neucha
  • Code: IBM Plex Mono

The fonts are built into the site, see Fontsource.

Design

The dark theme imitates graphite, the light one paper. Instead of straight lines there are pencil strokes.

I borrowed the ideas of a custom cursor and of the contents as dashes in the margin from Aaron Pham.

The tech radar is inspired by the Technology Radar by Thoughtworks.

Inspiration

I started taking notes a very long time ago. I want to thank Tseren Tserenov and Anatoly Levenchuk from MIM for helping me put new meaning into the method of thinking by writing.

Thanks to Aaron and Andy for their great sites, which I draw inspiration from for this place. By the way, I borrowed the idea of a colophon from Aaron too.
